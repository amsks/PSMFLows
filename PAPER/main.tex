\title{\textbf{PSMFlows}\\[0.3em]
\large Successor Measures over a Flow-Indexed Policy Family:\\
a step-by-step formulation}
\author{}
\date{Technical report --- 23 July 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Zero-shot reinforcement learning methods (FB, PSM) learn a reward-free representation by
training successor measures over a family of policies. Both families are defined in
\emph{action} space and both contain policies the offline dataset cannot execute: FB's
actor maximises over the whole action set, and PSM's codebook policies act uniformly at
random. On narrow-support data every Bellman backup is then evaluated off the data
distribution, and the damage is baked into a representation shared by all downstream
tasks.

This report develops the alternative in full. A behaviour-cloned conditional flow
$\Gth(s,\cdot)$ transports a Gaussian prior to the behaviour distribution, and we index
policies by the flow's \emph{noise} variable rather than by actions. The central result
(Proposition~\ref{prop:isometry}) is that this reparameterisation is an exact isometry of
behaviour-relative $f$-divergences: the unknown, state-dependent density ratio
$\pi/\mu$ equals the known, state-independent ratio $\nu/p_0$ pointwise. Concentrability
with respect to the behaviour policy --- the quantity offline RL spends its loss budget
controlling --- becomes a Gaussian-relative quantity available in closed form.

We then build successor measures over this family in PSM's affine form, derive the
bilinear implementation as a special case of a named assumption, prove the successor
feature identity that makes latent $Q$-iteration fully in-sample, and give a three-rung
zero-shot inference ladder with a generalised policy improvement guarantee. Claims we
cannot currently prove are stated as conjectures and marked \NOTPROVED.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting, notation, and the problem}
\label{sec:setting}

\subsection{Setting}

We work with a Markov decision process $(\Sspace,\Aspace,P,\gamma)$ with continuous
action set $\Aspace\subseteq\R^{d_a}$ and discount $\gamma\in[0,1)$. \emph{No reward is
specified at training time.} We are given a fixed offline dataset
\[
  \D=\{(s_i,a_i,s_i')\}_{i=1}^N,
\]
generated by an unknown behaviour policy $\mu(a\mid s)$, with state marginal $\rho$. We
write $\Dset(\Omega)$ for the probability measures on $\Omega$ and assume throughout that
$\mu(\cdot\mid s)$ admits a density with respect to Lebesgue measure on $\R^{d_a}$ for
$\rho$-a.e.\ $s$.

\begin{definition}[Successor measure]
\label{def:sm}
For a policy $\pi$, an initial state $s$ and a first action $a$, the discounted
successor measure is
\[
  M^\pi(s,a,X)\;=\;\E\Big[\textstyle\sum_{t\ge 1}\gamma^{t-1}\,\1_{\{s_t\in X\}}
    \;\Big|\; s_0=s,\;a_0=a,\;\pi\Big],
  \qquad X\subseteq\Sspace \text{ measurable}.
\]
\end{definition}

\noindent
For any reward $r:\Sspace\to\R$ received on arrival, $Q^\pi_r(s,a)=\int r(x)\,M^\pi(s,a,\dd x)$;
a single object therefore answers every reward at once, which is the entire premise of
zero-shot RL.

\begin{remark}[Convention]
\label{rem:convention}
Definition~\ref{def:sm} sums from $t\ge 1$, so the point mass in the Bellman recursion sits
at the \emph{successor} state $s'$. The alternative convention
$\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^t\1_{\{s_t\in X\}}$ places it at $s$ and changes
Proposition~\ref{prop:sf-identity} to $\psi(s,u,u')=\varphi(s)+\gamma\E_{s'}\psi(s',u',u')$.
We use the $t\ge1$ form because it is what FB uses and what the implemented loss of
\S\ref{sec:losses} computes; mixing the two is a common source of off-by-one errors in
this literature.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The problem, stated as a concentrability problem}
\label{sec:problem}

Every method in this family must, during training, evaluate its measure or critic at
successor state--action pairs $(s',a')$ where $a'$ is chosen by the algorithm rather than
drawn from the data. The resulting error is governed by a \emph{concentrability
coefficient}: writing $\pi_{\mathrm{tr}}$ for the distribution over $a'$ that the training
loop actually queries,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:concentrability}
  C(\pi_{\mathrm{tr}})\;=\;\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{s,\,a}
     \frac{\pi_{\mathrm{tr}}(a\mid s)}{\mu(a\mid s)} .
\end{equation}
Standard offline RL analyses bound the excess value of the learned object by a quantity
proportional to $C(\pi_{\mathrm{tr}})$; when $C$ is infinite, no such bound exists, and in
practice the model extrapolates confidently into regions it has never seen.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{FB.} The actor solves $\max_a F(s,a,z)^\top z$ over all of $\Aspace$. The
  resulting $\pi_{\mathrm{tr}}$ is deterministic and unrelated to $\mu$, so
  $C=\infty$.
\item \textbf{PSM.} The basis is trained on hash-codebook policies
  $\pi_z(s)=\mathrm{UniformSample}(z+\mathrm{hash}(s))$, i.e.\
  $\pi_{\mathrm{tr}}=\mathrm{Unif}(\Aspace)$, giving
  $C=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_a 1/(|\Aspace|\,\mu(a\mid s))$. This is finite only when
  $\mu$ has full support and is bounded away from zero --- exactly what broad-coverage
  datasets provide and narrow ones do not.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
Both methods therefore work on ExORL-style data and degrade when coverage narrows. The
degradation is qualitatively worse than in single-task offline RL, because the corrupted
object is a \emph{shared representation}: it fails on all downstream tasks
simultaneously.

Two families of fixes exist. Conservatism penalties (CQL, VC-FB/MC-FB) leave the policy
family alone and add a loss term that estimates and penalises the divergence in
\eqref{eq:concentrability}. Behavioural priors (FB-CPR) regularise softly toward a
dataset. This report takes a third route: \emph{change the policy family so that
\eqref{eq:concentrability} is computable in closed form and equal to one by
construction.}

\section{The behaviour flow and its inverse}
\label{sec:flow}

\subsection{Conditional flow matching}

Let $p_0=\mathcal N(0,I_{d_a})$ on $\R^{d_a}$ --- note $d_u = d_a$ throughout, which is
what makes the map below invertible. For $(s,a)\sim\D$, $u_0\sim p_0$, $u_1=a$ and
$t\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]$, set $u_t=(1-t)u_0+tu_1$, whose velocity along the interpolation
path is $\dot u_t=u_1-u_0=a-u_0$. We fit a conditional vector field
$v_\theta:\Sspace\times[0,1]\times\R^{d_a}\to\R^{d_a}$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:flow-loss}
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{flow}}(\theta)=
  \E_{(s,a)\sim\D,\;u_0\sim p_0,\;t\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]}
  \Big[\big\|v_\theta(s,t,u_t)-(a-u_0)\big\|_2^2\Big].
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Decoder and encoder]
\label{def:decoder}
$\Gth(s,u)$ is the value at $t=1$ of the solution to the initial value problem
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ode}
  \frac{\dd u_t}{\dd t}=v_\theta(s,t,u_t),\qquad u_{t=0}=u,
\end{equation}
and $\Eth(s,\cdot)=\Gth(s,\cdot)^{-1}$ is obtained by integrating \eqref{eq:ode} backward
from $t=1$ to $t=0$. We write $J(s,u)=\partial\Gth(s,u)/\partial u\in\R^{d_a\times d_a}$
and $J_t(s,u)=\partial u_t/\partial u$, so $J=J_1$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Regularity, invertibility, and the Jacobian}

\begin{assumption}[Regularity of the field]
\label{ass:lipschitz}
For $\rho$-a.e.\ $s$: $v_\theta(s,\cdot,\cdot)$ is continuous in $t$, continuously
differentiable in $u$, and globally Lipschitz in $u$ uniformly in $t\in[0,1]$, with
constant $L_s$.
\end{assumption}

\noindent
Assumption~\ref{ass:lipschitz} is a statement about the trained network, not about the
data; it holds for the standard MLP parameterisations with Lipschitz activations and
bounded weights.

\begin{lemma}[Diffeomorphism and Liouville formula]
\label{lem:diffeo}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:lipschitz}, for $\rho$-a.e.\ $s$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \eqref{eq:ode} has a unique solution on $[0,1]$ for every initial condition,
  and $\Gth(s,\cdot)$ is a diffeomorphism of class $C^1$ from $\R^{d_a}$ \emph{onto} itself,
  with inverse $\Eth(s,\cdot)$ obtained by backward integration;
\item[(ii)] the Jacobian satisfies the variational equation
  $\dot J_t=\partial_u v_\theta(s,t,u_t)\,J_t$ with $J_0=I$, and
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:liouville}
    \det J(s,u)=\exp\Big(\int_0^1\tr\,\partial_u v_\theta\big(s,t,u_t\big)\dd t\Big)>0 .
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) Global Lipschitz continuity in $u$, uniform in $t$, gives existence and uniqueness on
all of $[0,1]$ by Picard--Lindel\"of, with no finite-time blow-up. The flow map of an ODE
is invertible because integrating the same field backward from $u_1$ recovers $u_0$
uniquely, again by uniqueness of solutions; surjectivity onto $\R^{d_a}$ follows because
backward integration from an arbitrary endpoint is itself well posed and produces a
preimage. Continuous differentiability of the flow in its initial condition is the
standard smooth-dependence theorem, which applies since $\partial_u v_\theta$ exists and
is continuous.

(ii) Differentiating \eqref{eq:ode} in $u$ and exchanging derivatives (justified by
$C^1$ dependence) gives the variational equation. Liouville's formula for linear systems
states $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\det J_t=\tr\big(\partial_u v_\theta\big)\det J_t$; integrating
from $\det J_0=1$ gives \eqref{eq:liouville}, which is a positive exponential and in
particular never vanishes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Consequence that must not be over-read]
\label{rem:full-support}
Lemma~\ref{lem:diffeo}(i) says the image of $\Gth(s,\cdot)$ is \emph{all} of $\R^{d_a}$.
Consequently $\supp\big(\Gth(s,\cdot)_\#p_0\big)=\R^{d_a}$, and the statement ``every
decoded action lies in $\supp\mu(\cdot\mid s)$'' is \emph{false} as written. Every
guarantee below is therefore a statement about \emph{probability mass} or about
\emph{distributions}, never about topological support. See
Conjecture~\ref{conj:compact} for the tension this creates with compactly supported
behaviour distributions.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The flow behaviour model}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:muhat}
The \emph{flow behaviour model} is $\muhat(\cdot\mid s):=\Gth(s,\cdot)_\#p_0$, the
pushforward of the prior through the decoder.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Exact flow]
\label{ass:exact}
$\muhat(\cdot\mid s)=\mu(\cdot\mid s)$ for $\rho$-a.e.\ $s$.
\end{assumption}

\noindent
Assumption~\ref{ass:exact} is the idealisation under which the clean statements hold. It
is the minimiser of \eqref{eq:flow-loss} in the infinite-capacity, infinite-data limit. It
is \emph{not} attainable in the regime this report targets (Conjecture~\ref{conj:compact}),
and results are stated with respect to $\muhat$ wherever that costs nothing, so that the
flow-approximation error is visible rather than hidden.

\begin{lemma}[Change of variables]
\label{lem:cov}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:lipschitz}, $\muhat(\cdot\mid s)$ has density
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cov}
  \muhat(a\mid s)=\frac{p_0\big(\Eth(s,a)\big)}{\big|\det J\big(s,\Eth(s,a)\big)\big|} .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Lemma~\ref{lem:diffeo} and the change-of-variables formula for the
pushforward of a density under a $C^1$-diffeomorphism.
\end{proof}

\section{The flow-indexed policy family}
\label{sec:family}

\subsection{Three nested classes}

\begin{definition}[Latent and fixed-index policies]
\label{def:classes}
For a state-conditioned latent distribution $\nu(\cdot\mid s)\in\Dset(\R^{d_a})$, the
\emph{latent policy} $\pi_\nu$ acts by drawing $u\sim\nu(\cdot\mid s)$ and playing
$\Gth(s,u)$, i.e.\ $\pi_\nu(\cdot\mid s)=\Gth(s,\cdot)_\#\nu(\cdot\mid s)$. Write
$\Piclat=\{\pi_\nu\}$. Two distinguished subclasses:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \textbf{Fixed-index policies} $\pi_u(s)=\Gth(s,u)$, one deterministic Markov
  policy for each $u\in\R^{d_a}$ ($\nu=\delta_u$, the same $u$ at every state). This is the
  \emph{indexing family} over which the representation is trained.
\item[(b)] \textbf{State-wise greedy policies} $\nu(\cdot\mid s)=\delta_{u^\star(s)}$,
  produced at inference time. These are the policies actually deployed.
\end{enumerate}
Note $\nu=p_0$ recovers $\muhat$ itself.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Why $\Piclat$ is not the interesting object]
\label{rem:vacuous}
Since $\Gth(s,\cdot)$ is a bijection (Lemma~\ref{lem:diffeo}), \emph{every} policy on
$\R^{d_a}$ equals $\pi_\nu$ for $\nu=(\Gth(s,\cdot)^{-1})_\#\pi(\cdot\mid s)$. So
$\Piclat$ is the set of \emph{all} policies, so any claim that it is exactly the
well-covered class is vacuous. The content of the reparameterisation is not
\emph{which} policies it can express but \emph{how it measures} them, which is the subject
of the next subsection.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The divergence isometry}

\begin{proposition}[Behaviour-relative divergences are Gaussian-relative divergences]
\label{prop:isometry}
Let Assumption~\ref{ass:lipschitz} hold and let $\nu(\cdot\mid s)$ admit a density. Then
for $\rho$-a.e.\ $s$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \textup{(pointwise ratio identity)} for every $a\in\R^{d_a}$, writing
  $u=\Eth(s,a)$,
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ratio}
    \frac{\pi_\nu(a\mid s)}{\muhat(a\mid s)}\;=\;\frac{\nu(u\mid s)}{p_0(u)} ;
  \end{equation}
\item[(ii)] \textup{(isometry)} for every convex $f$ with $f(1)=0$,
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:isometry}
    D_f\big(\pi_\nu(\cdot\mid s)\,\big\|\,\muhat(\cdot\mid s)\big)
    \;=\;D_f\big(\nu(\cdot\mid s)\,\big\|\,p_0\big).
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:exact} one may replace $\muhat$ by $\mu$ throughout.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) By Lemma~\ref{lem:cov} applied to $\nu$ and to $p_0$ under the same map $\Gth(s,\cdot)$,
\[
  \pi_\nu(a\mid s)=\frac{\nu(u\mid s)}{|\det J(s,u)|},
  \qquad
  \muhat(a\mid s)=\frac{p_0(u)}{|\det J(s,u)|},
  \qquad u=\Eth(s,a).
\]
Both densities are evaluated at the same point $a$, hence at the same preimage $u$, hence
carry the \emph{same} Jacobian factor. Taking the ratio cancels it, and the factor is
nonzero by \eqref{eq:liouville}, so the division is legitimate.

(ii) Substituting $a=\Gth(s,u)$ and using $\muhat(\dd a\mid s)=p_0(\dd u)$ (definition of
pushforward),
\[
  D_f\big(\pi_\nu\|\muhat\big)
  =\int f\!\Big(\frac{\pi_\nu(a\mid s)}{\muhat(a\mid s)}\Big)\muhat(\dd a\mid s)
  \;\overset{(i)}{=}\;\int f\!\Big(\frac{\nu(u\mid s)}{p_0(u)}\Big)p_0(\dd u)
  =D_f\big(\nu\|p_0\big).\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\noindent
Equation~\eqref{eq:isometry} is the load-bearing statement of this report, so it is worth
saying plainly what it buys. The left-hand side involves $\mu$, which is unknown, is a
different distribution at every state, and has geometry nobody can characterise. The
right-hand side involves only $p_0=\mathcal N(0,I)$ and the latent distribution we choose.
Every behaviour-regularised objective of the form
$\max_\pi\, J(\pi)\ \text{s.t.}\ D_f(\pi\|\mu)\le\epsilon$ --- the shape that CQL, BRAC,
VC-FB and most of offline RL instantiate --- becomes
$\max_\nu\, J(\pi_\nu)\ \text{s.t.}\ D_f(\nu\|p_0)\le\epsilon$, whose constraint is
available in closed form for Gaussian $\nu$ and requires \emph{no density model of the
behaviour policy at all}. In particular, for $f(x)=x\log x$,
$\mathrm{KL}\big(\pi_\nu(\cdot\mid s)\|\mu(\cdot\mid s)\big)
=\mathrm{KL}\big(\nu(\cdot\mid s)\|\mathcal N(0,I)\big)$, exactly, at every state.

\begin{corollary}[Mass conversion]
\label{cor:mass}
For every measurable $U\subseteq\R^{d_a}$ and $\rho$-a.e.\ $s$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mass}
  \muhat\big(\Gth(s,U)\,\big|\,s\big)=p_0(U).
\end{equation}
In particular, for
$\Udelta:=\{u:\|u\|^2\le\chi^2_{d_a}(1-\delta)\}$ we have
$\muhat\big(\Gth(s,\Udelta)\mid s\big)=1-\delta$ \emph{simultaneously at every state},
where $\chi^2_{d_a}(1-\delta)$ is the $(1-\delta)$-quantile of the chi-squared
distribution with $d_a$ degrees of freedom.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\muhat(\Gth(s,U)\mid s)=p_0\big(\Gth(s,\cdot)^{-1}(\Gth(s,U))\big)=p_0(U)$ by the
definition of pushforward and bijectivity (Lemma~\ref{lem:diffeo}). The second claim is
$\|u\|^2\sim\chi^2_{d_a}$ under $p_0$.
\end{proof}

\noindent
This is the precise version of ``the flow converts a hard constraint into an easy one''.
The constraint ``$a$ carries at least $1-\delta$ of the behaviour mass at $s$'' is
state-dependent with unknown geometry; its latent image is a \emph{single Euclidean ball,
the same at every state, known before training begins}.

\begin{corollary}[Bounded concentrability for stochastic latent policies]
\label{cor:conc}
If $\|\dd\nu(\cdot\mid s)/\dd p_0\|_\infty\le\kappa$ for $\rho$-a.e.\ $s$, then
$C(\pi_\nu)\le\kappa$ in the sense of \eqref{eq:concentrability} with $\mu$ replaced by
$\muhat$. Taking $\nu=p_0$ gives $C=1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from \eqref{eq:ratio} and the definition \eqref{eq:concentrability}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The two regimes must not be conflated]
\label{rem:two-regimes}
Corollaries~\ref{cor:mass} and \ref{cor:conc} govern different objects and the distinction
is load-bearing.
\begin{itemize}
\item A \emph{deterministic} policy --- both the fixed-index $\pi_u$ of
  Definition~\ref{def:classes}(a) and the deployed greedy policy of
  Definition~\ref{def:classes}(b) --- has $\nu=\delta_u\not\ll p_0$ and therefore
  \emph{infinite} $f$-divergence from $\mu$. Corollary~\ref{cor:conc} says nothing about
  it. Its governing guarantee is the mass statement, Corollary~\ref{cor:mass}: confine
  $u$ to $\Udelta$ and the action carries $1-\delta$ of the behaviour mass.
\item \emph{At training time} the fixed-index family is never used in isolation: the loss
  of \S\ref{sec:losses} averages over $u'\sim p_0$, so the marginal law of the bootstrap
  actions is exactly $\muhat(\cdot\mid s')$ and the operator's concentrability is $1$.
  That is the content of Proposition~\ref{prop:insample}, and it is a statement about the
  \emph{aggregate} training distribution, not about any individual $\pi_{u'}$.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Density-adapted resolution]
\label{cor:resolution}
For $\rho$-a.e.\ $s$ and every $u$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:resolution}
  \big|\det J(s,u)\big|=\frac{p_0(u)}{\muhat\big(\Gth(s,u)\mid s\big)} .
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Rearrange \eqref{eq:cov} at $a=\Gth(s,u)$.
\end{proof}

\noindent
Equation~\eqref{eq:resolution} makes the ``implicit trust region'' claim quantitative: the
local volume expansion of the decoder is exactly the ratio of prior density to behaviour
density. Where the data is dense the map contracts, so a fixed step in $u$ moves the
action little; across low-density gaps it expands, so the same step jumps over the gap.
Any optimiser working at fixed resolution in $u$ therefore searches finely inside data
modes and coarsely between them, without being told where the modes are.

\subsection{Transport coherence: when is the indexing family useful?}

Nothing so far guarantees that a \emph{fixed} $u$ selects behaviourally consistent actions
across states. If it does not, the family $\{\pi_u\}$ is a collection of incoherent
policies and, while every result above still holds, the representation has little
structure to exploit. We make this measurable.

\begin{definition}[Transport coherence]
\label{def:coherence}
Let $\{C_k(s)\}_{k=1}^m$ be a measurable partition of $\R^{d_a}$ for each $s$ (in practice:
the cells of a mode decomposition of $\muhat(\cdot\mid s)$, e.g.\ mixture components or
$k$-means cells of the dataset actions). The decoder $\Gth$ is \emph{$\varepsilon$-coherent}
with respect to $\{C_k\}$ if there exists a labelling $\kappa:\R^{d_a}\to\{1,\dots,m\}$
with
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:coherence}
  \big(\rho\otimes p_0\big)\Big\{(s,u)\;:\;\Gth(s,u)\notin C_{\kappa(u)}(s)\Big\}\;\le\;\varepsilon .
\end{equation}
That is, a fixed latent lands in the same-labelled mode at all but an $\varepsilon$-fraction
of state--latent pairs.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Coherence]
\label{ass:coherence}
$\Gth$ is $\varepsilon$-coherent with $\varepsilon\ll (m-1)/m$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}[What Assumption~\ref{ass:coherence} is and is not needed for]
\label{rem:coherence-role}
Assumption~\ref{ass:coherence} is used by \emph{none} of
Propositions~\ref{prop:isometry}, \ref{prop:sf-identity}, \ref{prop:insample} or
\ref{prop:gpi}: soundness of the representation and of the backups does not depend on it.
It is a hypothesis about \emph{statistical efficiency}. If $\varepsilon$ approaches the
chance level $(m-1)/m$, then a fixed $u$ selects modes essentially at random across states,
the successor measures $\{M^{\pi_u}\}$ all concentrate near $M^{\muhat}$, the family spans
a low-dimensional and uninformative set, and the basis dimension $d_z$ needed to
distinguish useful policies grows. The quantity $\varepsilon$ in \eqref{eq:coherence} is
directly estimable from rollouts (diagnostic D2), so Assumption~\ref{ass:coherence} is
falsifiable before any representation is trained. If it fails, the mitigation is to
re-index the family --- learn $\nu_\eta(u\mid s,z)$ for a low-dimensional $z$ with a
mutual-information term --- which preserves every result in
\S\ref{sec:family} because those results hold for arbitrary $\nu$.
\end{remark}

\section{Successor measures over the flow family}
\label{sec:sm}

\subsection{The two-argument object and its Bellman equation}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:Muu}
For $u,u'\in\R^{d_a}$, let $M^{u\to u'}(s,\cdot)$ denote the successor measure from $s$
when the first action is $\Gth(s,u)$ and the continuation policy is $\pi_{u'}$:
\[
  M^{u\to u'}(s,\cdot)\;=\;M^{\pi_{u'}}\big(s,\Gth(s,u),\cdot\big)
\]
in the notation of Definition~\ref{def:sm}. We write $m(s,u,u',x)$ for its density with
respect to $\rho$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Bellman equation; diagonal closure]
\label{lem:bellman}
For all $s,u,u'$ and measurable $X$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bellman}
  M^{u\to u'}(s,X)=\E_{s'\sim P(\cdot\mid s,\Gth(s,u))}
  \Big[\1_{\{s'\in X\}}+\gamma\,M^{u'\to u'}(s',X)\Big].
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Split the sum of Definition~\ref{def:sm} at $t=1$ and apply the Markov property. After the
first transition the agent follows $\pi_{u'}$, so its first action at $s'$ is
$\Gth(s',u')$ and its continuation is again $\pi_{u'}$; both arguments of the successor
object are therefore $u'$.
\end{proof}

\noindent
The recursion \emph{closes on the diagonal}: only $M^{u'\to u'}$ appears on the right. The
two-argument object is exactly the $Q$-like one-step extension of the $V$-like diagonal
object, in the same relation as $(Q^\pi,V^\pi)$, and this is what makes a single learned
network sufficient.

\subsection{Affine representation}

\begin{assumption}[Affine successor-measure form]
\label{ass:affine}
There exist a bias $b$, a basis $\Phi$ taking values in $\R^{d_z}$, and coordinates
$w^{u'}\in\R^{d_z}$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:affine}
  m(s,u,u',x)\;=\;b(s,u,x)\;+\;\Phi(s,u,x)^\top w^{u'}
\end{equation}
for all $s,u,u',x$, where $b$ and $\Phi$ \emph{do not depend on the policy index} $u'$.
\end{assumption}

\noindent
Assumption~\ref{ass:affine} is PSM's affine decomposition instantiated on the flow family:
the policy enters the successor measure only through a finite-dimensional coordinate. PSM
proves the corresponding statement for finite MDPs over all policies; the version we need
--- restricted to $\{\pi_{u'}\}$ --- is Conjecture~\ref{conj:span}.

Substituting \eqref{eq:affine} into \eqref{eq:bellman} gives the constraint the learned
objects must satisfy:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bellman-affine}
  b(s,u,x)+\Phi(s,u,x)^\top w^{u'}
  =\E_{s'}\Big[\tfrac{\delta_{s'}(\dd x)}{\rho(\dd x)}
   +\gamma\big(b(s',u',x)+\Phi(s',u',x)^\top w^{u'}\big)\Big],
\end{equation}
with $s'\sim P(\cdot\mid s,\Gth(s,u))$. Note the asymmetry: the left side is evaluated at
index $u$, the right at $u'$, which is precisely the diagonal closure of
Lemma~\ref{lem:bellman}.

\begin{assumption}[Factorised basis]
\label{ass:factorised}
There exist $\varphi:\Sspace\to\R^{d}$, $A:\Sspace\times\R^{d_a}\to\R^{d_z\times d}$ and
$\beta:\Sspace\times\R^{d_a}\to\R^{d}$ with
\[
  \Phi(s,u,x)=A(s,u)\,\varphi(x),
  \qquad
  b(s,u,x)=\beta(s,u)^\top\varphi(x).
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[The bilinear form is a special case]
\label{prop:bilinear}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:affine} and \ref{ass:factorised},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bilinear}
  m(s,u,u',x)=\psi(s,u,u')^\top\varphi(x),
  \qquad
  \psi(s,u,u')=A(s,u)^\top w^{u'}+\beta(s,u),
\end{equation}
so $m$ is bilinear in $(\psi,\varphi)$ and $\psi$ is \emph{affine in the policy coordinate}
$w^{u'}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$b+\Phi^\top w^{u'}=\beta(s,u)^\top\varphi(x)+\big(A(s,u)\varphi(x)\big)^\top w^{u'}
=\big(\beta(s,u)+A(s,u)^\top w^{u'}\big)^\top\varphi(x)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the implementation keeps and what it gives up]
\label{rem:tradeoff}
The implemented agent parameterises $\psi(s,u,u')$ as a free network, i.e.\ it adopts
\eqref{eq:bilinear} but \emph{not} the affineness of $\psi$ in $w^{u'}$. The consequences
are worth stating explicitly.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
 & affine \eqref{eq:affine} & factorised \eqref{eq:bilinear} & free $\psi$ (implemented) \\
\midrule
policy-independent basis        & $\Phi,b$        & $\varphi$      & $\varphi$ \\
policy index                    & $w^{u'}$        & $w^{u'}$       & absorbed into $\psi$ \\
closed-form reward inference    & yes             & yes            & yes \\
constrained (LP) inference over $w$ & yes         & yes            & \textbf{no} \\
GPI guarantee (Prop.~\ref{prop:gpi})& yes         & yes            & yes \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The constrained-optimisation inference of full PSM works \emph{only because} $m$ is linear
in the task coordinate; a free $\psi$ forfeits it and retains closed-form $w$ only. This is
a deliberate trade, not an oversight.
\end{remark}

\section{The successor-feature identity and latent \texorpdfstring{$Q$}{Q}-iteration}
\label{sec:sf}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:sf}
The successor features of the flow family are
$\psi(s,u,u')=\int\varphi(x)\,M^{u\to u'}(s,\dd x)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Successor-feature Bellman identity]
\label{prop:sf-identity}
For all $s,u,u'$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sf-identity}
  \psi(s,u,u')=\E_{s'\sim P(\cdot\mid s,\Gth(s,u))}
  \Big[\varphi(s')+\gamma\,\psi(s',u',u')\Big].
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Integrate \eqref{eq:bellman} against $\varphi$ and exchange integrals (Fubini; $\varphi$
bounded, $M$ finite since $\gamma<1$).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Reward identity: the draft conjecture, settled]
\label{cor:reward-identity}
For any $w\in\R^{d}$, with $\hat r_w(x):=\varphi(x)^\top w$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reward-identity}
  \E_{s'}\Big[\big(\psi(s,u,u')-\gamma\,\psi(s',u',u')\big)^\top w\Big]
  \;=\;\E_{s'}\big[\hat r_w(s')\big],
\end{equation}
which is independent of the continuation index $u'$ --- and indeed of $u$ except through
the transition kernel.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take inner products of \eqref{eq:sf-identity} with $w$ and rearrange.
\end{proof}

\noindent
An earlier draft of this work conjectured \eqref{eq:reward-identity} with the remark ``if
I'm not completely mistaken''. The conjecture is correct, it is nothing more than the
successor-feature Bellman identity, and it holds for \emph{every} $u'$ rather than
approximately. Two consequences follow.

\begin{corollary}[Closed-form reward inference]
\label{cor:reward-inference}
If $\E_{x\sim\rho}[\varphi(x)\varphi(x)^\top]=I_d$, then the least-squares projection of a
reward $r$ onto the basis is $w=\E_{x\sim\rho}\big[r(x)\varphi(x)\big]$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Minimising $\E_\rho[(r-\varphi^\top w)^2]$ gives the normal equations
$\E_\rho[\varphi\varphi^\top]w=\E_\rho[r\varphi]$; the assumed orthonormality reduces the
left side to $w$.
\end{proof}

\noindent
Corollary~\ref{cor:reward-inference} explains the role of the orthonormality regulariser
in \S\ref{sec:losses}: it is not a stabilisation heuristic but the condition under which
the closed-form $w$ is the correct least-squares answer.

\begin{corollary}[Latent $Q$-iteration]
\label{cor:latent-q}
Fix $w$ and a latent action set $\mathcal U\subseteq\R^{d_a}$. The optimal value over
state-wise latent selection satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:latent-q}
  Q^\star_w(s,u)=\E_{s'\sim P(\cdot\mid s,\Gth(s,u))}
   \Big[\hat r_w(s')+\gamma\max_{u'\in\mathcal U}Q^\star_w(s',u')\Big].
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\eqref{eq:latent-q} is the Bellman optimality equation of the MDP whose action set is
$\mathcal U$, whose transition kernel is $P(\cdot\mid s,\Gth(s,u))$, and whose reward is
$\hat r_w$ received on arrival. The right-hand side defines a $\gamma$-contraction in
$\|\cdot\|_\infty$ by the standard argument, so the fixed point exists and is unique.
\end{proof}

\noindent
This is an ordinary $Q$-learning problem posed in the latent variable. The maximisation is
over $u'$, and every $u'$ decodes to an action drawn from the flow behaviour model, so no
out-of-distribution action query appears anywhere in the loop. Taking $\mathcal U=\Udelta$
gives, by Corollary~\ref{cor:mass}, an explicit $1-\delta$ behaviour-mass guarantee on the
resulting greedy policy at every state.

\section{Dataset latents: inverting the flow}
\label{sec:preimage}

The updates of \S\ref{sec:losses} require triples $(s,u,s')$ with
$s'\sim P(\cdot\mid s,\Gth(s,u))$, but the dataset supplies $(s,a,s')$. We need a latent
$u$ with $\Gth(s,u)=a$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:diffeo} there is exactly one, namely
$u=\Eth(s,a)$, computable by backward integration.

\begin{lemma}[Inverted latents are prior-distributed]
\label{lem:typicality}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:lipschitz} and \ref{ass:exact}, if $(s,a)\sim\D$ then
$\Eth(s,a)\sim p_0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$a\sim\mu(\cdot\mid s)=\muhat(\cdot\mid s)=\Gth(s,\cdot)_\#p_0$; pushing forward through
$\Eth(s,\cdot)=\Gth(s,\cdot)^{-1}$ returns $p_0$.
\end{proof}

\noindent
Lemma~\ref{lem:typicality} turns diagnostic D3 into a direct test of
Assumption~\ref{ass:exact}: comparing the empirical distribution of $\|\Eth(s,a)\|^2$
against $\chi^2_{d_a}$ tests exactness of the flow, not merely numerical health of the
integrator. If inverted latents are atypical under $p_0$, then the latents seen at training
time and the samples $u'\sim p_0$ used at evaluation time come from different
distributions, and the representation is being trained and queried on different families.

Because the trained flow is imperfect, we replace the point preimage by the
$\varepsilon$-relaxed posterior
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:posterior}
  q_\alpha(u\mid s,a)\;\propto\;p_0(u)\,
  \exp\!\big(-\alpha\|\Gth(s,u)-a\|^2\big),\qquad \alpha>0 .
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Laplace approximation of the preimage posterior]
\label{prop:laplace}
Let $u^\star=\Eth(s,a)$ and $J=J(s,u^\star)$. The second-order expansion of
\eqref{eq:posterior} about $u^\star$ is $\mathcal N(m_\alpha,\Sigma_\alpha)$ with
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:laplace}
  \Sigma_\alpha=\big(I+2\alpha J^\top J\big)^{-1},
  \qquad
  m_\alpha=2\alpha\,\Sigma_\alpha J^\top J\,u^\star .
\end{equation}
As $\alpha\to\infty$, $m_\alpha\to u^\star$ and
$\Sigma_\alpha\to(2\alpha)^{-1}(J^\top J)^{-1}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\delta=u-u^\star$. A first-order expansion of the decoder gives
$\Gth(s,u)-a=J\delta+O(\|\delta\|^2)$, so the likelihood term is
$\exp(-\alpha\delta^\top J^\top J\delta)=\exp(-\tfrac12\delta^\top(2\alpha J^\top J)\delta)$,
a Gaussian factor with precision $2\alpha J^\top J$. The prior $p_0=\mathcal N(0,I)$ has
precision $I$. Multiplying Gaussians adds precisions, giving
$\Sigma_\alpha^{-1}=I+2\alpha J^\top J$, and the mean is $\Sigma_\alpha$ times the sum of
precision-weighted means, $2\alpha J^\top J u^\star+I\cdot 0$. For the limit,
$(I+2\alpha J^\top J)^{-1}2\alpha J^\top J\to I$ as $\alpha\to\infty$ whenever $J$ is
nonsingular, which holds by \eqref{eq:liouville}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The posterior spread is the local geometry]
\label{rem:laplace-geometry}
Pushing $\Sigma_\alpha\to(2\alpha)^{-1}(J^\top J)^{-1}$ forward through the linearised
decoder gives covariance $J\Sigma_\alpha J^\top\to(2\alpha)^{-1}I$ in \emph{action} space.
So \eqref{eq:posterior} is an isotropic ball of radius $\sim(2\alpha)^{-1/2}$ around $a$,
pulled back through the flow. Its anisotropy in latent space is not a modelling choice but
the inverse Jacobian geometry: the posterior is wide in $u$ exactly where the decoder
contracts, i.e.\ where the data is dense (Corollary~\ref{cor:resolution}). The Gaussian
mixture fitted by EM in the implementation is a multi-modal refinement of this Laplace
object, not an unrelated heuristic.
\end{remark}

\section{In-sample temporal-difference learning}
\label{sec:insample}

\begin{definition}[The PSMFlows TD operator]
\label{def:operator}
For a candidate $m$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:operator}
  (\mathcal T m)(s,u,u',x)
  =\E_{s'\sim P(\cdot\mid s,\Gth(s,u))}
   \Big[\tfrac{\delta_{s'}(\dd x)}{\rho(\dd x)}+\gamma\,m(s',u',u',x)\Big],
\end{equation}
where at $s'$ the bootstrap action is $\Gth(s',u')$ with $u'\sim p_0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[In-sample property]
\label{prop:insample}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:exact}, if $s'\sim\rho$ and $u'\sim p_0$ independently, then the
bootstrap action satisfies $\Gth(s',u')\sim\mu(\cdot\mid s')$. Consequently the joint law
of the pairs $(s',a')$ at which $\mathcal T$ evaluates $m$ is exactly the data distribution
$\rho\otimes\mu$, and the concentrability coefficient \eqref{eq:concentrability} of the
operator equals $1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\Gth(s',\cdot)_\#p_0=\muhat(\cdot\mid s')=\mu(\cdot\mid s')$ by
Definition~\ref{def:muhat} and Assumption~\ref{ass:exact}. Independence of $u'$ and $s'$
gives the joint law, and \eqref{eq:concentrability} is then a ratio of identical densities.
\end{proof}

\noindent
Proposition~\ref{prop:insample} is the formal separation from FB and PSM. Placing it beside
\S\ref{sec:problem}:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Method & bootstrap action distribution & $C(\pi_{\mathrm{tr}})$ \\
\midrule
FB      & $\delta_{\argmax_a F(s',a,z)^\top z}$ & $\infty$ \\
PSM     & $\mathrm{Unif}(\Aspace)$              & $\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_a 1/(|\Aspace|\mu(a\mid s'))$ \\
PSMFlows& $\Gth(s',\cdot)_\#p_0=\muhat(\cdot\mid s')$ & $1$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{proposition}[Contraction and fixed-point error]
\label{prop:contraction}
$\mathcal T$ is a $\gamma$-contraction in $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ on bounded measurable
functions, with unique fixed point $m(s,u,u',\cdot)=M^{u\to u'}(s,\cdot)/\rho$. If a
learned $\hat m$ satisfies $\|\hat m-\mathcal T\hat m\|_\infty\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{TD}}$
then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fixed-point}
  \|\hat m-m\|_\infty\;\le\;\frac{\epsilon_{\mathrm{TD}}}{1-\gamma}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For bounded $m_1,m_2$, $\|\mathcal Tm_1-\mathcal Tm_2\|_\infty\le\gamma\|m_1-m_2\|_\infty$
since the point-mass term cancels and the expectation is an average. Lemma~\ref{lem:bellman}
identifies the fixed point. For \eqref{eq:fixed-point},
$\|\hat m-m\|\le\|\hat m-\mathcal T\hat m\|+\|\mathcal T\hat m-\mathcal Tm\|
\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{TD}}+\gamma\|\hat m-m\|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What is and is not new here]
\label{rem:contraction-novelty}
Proposition~\ref{prop:contraction} is entirely standard; contraction is never the difficult
part. What Proposition~\ref{prop:insample} adds is that $\epsilon_{\mathrm{TD}}$ is
estimable \emph{from the dataset alone}: the Bellman residual is evaluated at pairs drawn
from $\rho\otimes\mu$, which is what the data provides. For FB and PSM the corresponding
residual is evaluated at pairs the data does not contain, so the empirical residual is not
an estimate of $\epsilon_{\mathrm{TD}}$ at all, and the bound requires a concentrability
factor over $\Aspace$ that is unbounded on narrow data. A sample-complexity statement
making this quantitative in the continuous case is Conjecture~\ref{conj:insample-bound};
in the finite-state, finite-latent-codebook case it follows from the standard in-sample
offline RL analyses with state-coverage $C_\rho$ in place of state--action coverage.
\end{remark}

\section{Training objective}
\label{sec:losses}

\subsection{Derivation}

We fit $m$ by minimising the $L^2(\rho)$ distance to its Bellman target, with a stop-gradient
target $\bar m$:
\[
  \mathcal L(m)=\E_{x\sim\rho}\Big[\big(m(s,u,u',x)-(\mathcal T\bar m)(s,u,u',x)\big)^2\Big].
\]
Expanding the square and using
$\E_{x\sim\rho}\big[m(\cdot,x)\,\tfrac{\delta_{s'}(\dd x)}{\rho(\dd x)}\big]=m(\cdot,s')$,
all terms not depending on $m$ are constants, leaving (writing $\overset{c}{=}$ for
equality up to an additive constant independent of $m$)
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:loss-abstract}
  \mathcal L(m)\;\overset{c}{=}\;
  \E_{x\sim\rho}\Big[\big(m(s,u,u',x)-\gamma\,\bar m(s',u',u',x)\big)^2\Big]
  \;-\;2\,\E_{s'}\big[m(s,u,u',s')\big].
\end{equation}
The first term is an ordinary squared Bellman residual over a random ``negative'' state
$x\sim\rho$; the second is a ``positive'' term rewarding mass at the observed successor.
This is the norm-squared measure-matching loss of FB and PSM, here instantiated on the
flow family.

\subsection{Empirical objective}

Let $\{(s_i,a_i,s_i')\}_{i=1}^B$ be a batch, $u_i\sim q_\alpha(\cdot\mid s_i,a_i)$ the
inverted latents of \S\ref{sec:preimage}, $u'\sim p_0$ a fresh index per batch element, and
$\bar\psi,\bar\varphi$ target networks. Using the bilinear form \eqref{eq:bilinear} and
drawing negatives $x=s_j'$ from other batch elements:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:loss-empirical}
\boxed{\;
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{SM}}
  =\;&\frac{1}{B^2}\sum_{i,j}\Big(\psi(s_i,u_i,u')^\top\varphi(s_j')
      -\gamma\,\bar\psi(s_i',u',u')^\top\bar\varphi(s_j')\Big)^2 \\
   &-\frac{2}{B}\sum_i \psi(s_i,u_i,u')^\top\varphi(s_i')
   \;+\;\lambda_\perp\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ortho}}(\varphi),
\end{aligned}\;}
\end{equation}
with
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ortho}}(\varphi)=
\big\|\tfrac1B\sum_i\varphi(s_i)\varphi(s_i)^\top-I_d\big\|_F^2$.

\begin{remark}[The load-bearing line]
\label{rem:load-bearing}
In \eqref{eq:loss-empirical} the bootstrap term evaluates $\bar\psi$ at the successor state
$s_i'$ with continuation index $u'$, whose decoded action is $\Gth(s_i',u')$ --- a sample
from $\muhat(\cdot\mid s_i')$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:insample}. \emph{Every action the
backup ever evaluates is a flow decode.} The corresponding line in PSM evaluates a uniform
random action; in FB, an unconstrained actor maximiser. Holding everything else fixed and
swapping only this line is the cleanest single-variable comparison available, and is the
ablation the experimental programme should run first.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Role of $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ortho}}$]
By Corollary~\ref{cor:reward-inference}, $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ortho}}$ enforces exactly the
condition $\E_\rho[\varphi\varphi^\top]=I$ under which the closed-form reward inference
$w=\E_\D[r\varphi]$ is the least-squares projection. It is part of the specification, not a
regulariser tuned for stability.
\end{remark}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{PSMFlows pretraining (reward-free)}
\label{alg:pretrain}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require dataset $\D$, prior $p_0$, discount $\gamma$, sharpness $\alpha$
\State \textbf{Stage A.} Fit $v_\theta$ by \eqref{eq:flow-loss}; \textbf{freeze} $\theta$.
\State \textbf{Stage B.} For each $(s_i,a_i)\in\D$ precompute $u^\star_i=\Eth(s_i,a_i)$ and
       $J(s_i,u^\star_i)$ by backward ODE; fit $q_\alpha(\cdot\mid s_i,a_i)$ via
       Proposition~\ref{prop:laplace} (EM refinement optional). Cache.
\State \textbf{Stage C.} \textbf{repeat}
\State \quad sample batch $\{(s_i,a_i,s_i')\}$, draw $u_i\sim q_\alpha(\cdot\mid s_i,a_i)$,
       draw $u'\sim p_0$
\State \quad update $(\psi,\varphi)$ by gradient descent on \eqref{eq:loss-empirical};
       update targets $\bar\psi,\bar\varphi$
\State \textbf{until} converged
\Ensure basis $\varphi$, successor features $\psi$, frozen decoder $\Gth$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent
Freezing $\theta$ after Stage A means the representation is trained against a
\emph{stationary} policy family. FB trains against a moving actor, so its measure targets
chase a family that changes underneath them; the resulting non-stationarity is a plausible
contributor to FB's seed variance and is worth measuring directly.

\section{Zero-shot inference: a three-rung ladder}
\label{sec:inference}

Given a reward $r$, set $w=\E_\D[r(x)\varphi(x)]$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:reward-inference}).
The three rungs trade computation at task time against optimality.

\subsection{Rung 1 --- flow-GPI, no test-time training}

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{Rung 1: flow-GPI action selection}
\label{alg:gpi}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require state $s$, task code $w$, budget $K$, level $\delta$
\State draw $u'_1,\dots,u'_K\sim p_0$; draw $u_1,\dots,u_K\sim p_0$
       \Comment{each lies in $\Udelta$ w.p.\ $1-\delta$ (Cor.~\ref{cor:mass})}
\State $(\hat\imath,\hat\jmath)\gets\argmax_{i,j}\ \psi(s,u_i,u'_j)^\top w$
\State optionally refine $u_{\hat\imath}$ by a few gradient steps on
       $u\mapsto\psi(s,u,u'_{\hat\jmath})^\top w$, \textbf{projected onto} $\Udelta$
\State \Return $\Gth(s,u_{\hat\imath})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{proposition}[GPI over the flow continuum]
\label{prop:gpi}
Let $\Lambda_K=\{u'_1,\dots,u'_K\}$ and let $\pi^{\mathrm{GPI}}$ select at each $s$ the
action $\Gth(s,\hat u(s))$ with
$\hat u(s)\in\argmax_{u\in\Udelta}\max_{u'\in\Lambda_K}\psi(s,u,u')^\top w$. Suppose
\[
  \big|\psi(s,u,u')^\top w-Q^{\pi_{u'}}_r\big(s,\Gth(s,u)\big)\big|\;\le\;\epsilon
  \qquad\text{for all }s,u,u'\in\Lambda_K .
\]
Then for all $s,a$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gpi-bound}
  Q^{\pi^{\mathrm{GPI}}}_r(s,a)\;\ge\;\max_{u'\in\Lambda_K}Q^{\pi_{u'}}_r(s,a)
  \;-\;\frac{2\epsilon}{1-\gamma},
\end{equation}
and $\epsilon\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{flow}}+\dfrac{\epsilon_{\mathrm{TD}}}{1-\gamma}
+\dfrac{\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}}{1-\gamma}$ where $\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}=
\|r-\varphi^\top w\|_\infty$, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{TD}}$ is as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:contraction}, and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{flow}}$ bounds the value error
induced by $\muhat\ne\mu$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The bound \eqref{eq:gpi-bound} is the generalised policy improvement theorem of Barreto et
al.\ applied to the candidate set $\{\pi_{u'}\}_{u'\in\Lambda_K}$, whose action-value
functions are approximated to accuracy $\epsilon$ by $\psi^\top w$; the argument is
unchanged by the fact that the candidates are flow-indexed. The decomposition of $\epsilon$
is stated up to multiplicative constants depending on $\|w\|$ and $\|\varphi\|_\infty$,
which we absorb into the three terms; it is bookkeeping rather than a tight bound, and its
purpose is that each term is separately measurable. For it, the
reward-regression term contributes $\|Q_r-Q_{\hat r_w}\|_\infty\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{reg}}
/(1-\gamma)$ by the standard simulation bound; the measure error contributes
$\|\hat m-m\|_\infty\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{TD}}/(1-\gamma)$ by \eqref{eq:fixed-point},
scaled by $\|\varphi^\top w\|$; and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{flow}}$ collects the remaining
discrepancy from evaluating the family under $\muhat$ rather than $\mu$. Summing gives the
claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The candidate set is executable --- and the maximisation must be confined]
\label{rem:adversarial-u}
The distinguishing feature of \eqref{eq:gpi-bound} relative to USFA-style GPI is that every
policy in the supremum decodes through the flow, so the improvement is over a continuum of
\emph{behaviour-consistent} policies rather than an arbitrary set. This is also where the
scheme is most fragile: the outer maximisation over $u$ is an adversarial search against
the learned $\psi$, and an unconstrained search will find latents where $\psi$ is
optimistic and the flow is unreliable. Confining the search to $\Udelta$ is therefore not
a numerical convenience --- it is what converts Corollary~\ref{cor:mass} into a guarantee
about the deployed action. Diagnostic: report the gap between predicted
$\psi(s,\hat u,\cdot)^\top w$ and realised return; a widening gap as $K$ grows is the
signature of this failure.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Rung 2 --- in-sample latent \texorpdfstring{$Q$}{Q}-iteration}

Initialise $Q_w(s,u)\gets\max_{u'\in\Lambda_K}\psi(s,u,u')^\top w$ and iterate
\eqref{eq:latent-q} on the offline data with $\mathcal U=\Udelta$, using the cached latents
of \S\ref{sec:preimage} for the $(s,u)$ side and $u'\sim p_0$ for the max. No environment
interaction occurs.

\begin{proposition}[Rung 2 dominates Rung 1]
\label{prop:rung2}
Let $\Sspace$ be finite and $\mathcal U=\Lambda$ a finite latent codebook. Then the
iteration \eqref{eq:latent-q} converges to $Q^\star_w$, the optimal value over state-wise
latent selection on $\Lambda$, and the induced greedy policy satisfies
$Q^{\pi^\star}_{\hat r_w}\ge Q^{\pi_{u'}}_{\hat r_w}$ for every $u'\in\Lambda$ --- i.e.\ it
attains the target of \eqref{eq:gpi-bound} without the $2\epsilon/(1-\gamma)$ slack, up to
the error of the iteration itself.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Corollary~\ref{cor:latent-q} gives a finite MDP with action set $\Lambda$; value iteration
converges to its optimum by contraction. Every fixed-index policy $\pi_{u'}$ is the
constant-action policy $u'\equiv$ const in this MDP, hence is feasible, hence dominated by
the optimum.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Rung 3 --- amortised latent actor}

Distil $s\mapsto\argmax_{u\in\Udelta}Q_w(s,u)$ into $\pi_\eta(s,w)$ by regression across
sampled tasks $w$, giving single-forward-pass action selection at deployment. This rung
adds no guarantee; it trades a small distillation error for inference cost, and is the
behaviour-foundation-model interface.

\section{Conjectures and open problems}
\label{sec:open}

The results above are stated for the exact-flow idealisation
(Assumption~\ref{ass:exact}) and, where noted, for finite state and latent sets. The gap
between those statements and the deployed system is the following list. Each item is
labelled with what would close it.

\begin{conjecture}[Flow-error perturbation]
\label{conj:perturbation}
\NOTPROVED\;
Suppose $W_2\big(\muhat(\cdot\mid s),\mu(\cdot\mid s)\big)\le\varepsilon_s$ with
$\E_\rho[\varepsilon_s^2]\le\varepsilon^2$. Then Propositions~\ref{prop:isometry} and
\ref{prop:insample} hold up to an error controlled by $\varepsilon$.

\emph{Strategy and obstruction.} The transport statements degrade gracefully in $W_2$:
decoded actions are within $\varepsilon_s$-Wasserstein of behaviour samples, and the
value-error contribution is $O(\varepsilon L_Q/(1-\gamma))$ for $L_Q$-Lipschitz $Q$. The
\emph{divergence} statement is the difficulty: $f$-divergences are not continuous with
respect to $W_2$, so \eqref{eq:isometry} cannot be perturbed in this metric at all. Two
honest routes: (a) state the perturbed result only for smoothed divergences
$D_f(\pi*\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\,\|\,\mu*\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2))$, which are $W_2$-continuous
for fixed $\sigma$; or (b) assume a density-ratio bound
$\|\muhat/\mu\|_\infty\le 1+\eta$ directly, in which case
$D_f(\pi_\nu\|\mu)\le D_f(\nu\|p_0)+O(\eta)$ for $f$ with bounded second derivative. Route
(b) is a stronger assumption but yields the statement actually used. Neither is currently
carried out.

\emph{This conjecture blocks the honest version of Proposition~\ref{prop:isometry}}, which
is the report's headline result. It should be the first theoretical priority.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[Affine span over the flow family]
\label{conj:span}
\NOTPROVED\;
For a finite MDP, the successor measures $\{M^{\pi_u}:u\in\R^{d_a}\}$ of the fixed-index
family affinely span the successor measures of all policies in $\Piclat$ whose latent
distributions satisfy $\nu\ll p_0$. This is what justifies
Assumption~\ref{ass:affine} --- i.e.\ learning the basis on fixed-$u$ policies only ---
rather than positing it.

\emph{Strategy.} PSM's affine-space result establishes the analogous statement over all
policies of a finite MDP by exhibiting the successor-measure polytope. The restriction to
$\Piclat$ requires showing the fixed-index measures are not confined to a proper affine
subspace, which is where Assumption~\ref{ass:coherence} plausibly enters: an incoherent
family produces measures clustered near $M^{\muhat}$ and may fail to span. A finite-MDP
version suffices for the paper.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[Continuous-space in-sample error bound]
\label{conj:insample-bound}
\NOTPROVED\;
In the continuous case, the excess value of the greedy policy from
\eqref{eq:latent-q} is bounded by a quantity depending on the \emph{state}-coverage
coefficient $C_\rho=\|\dd\rho^{\pi}/\dd\rho\|_\infty$ alone, with no dependence on any
action-space concentrability coefficient.

\emph{Strategy.} Follow the in-sample analyses (IQL, In-Sample Softmax) with the latent
action set $\Udelta$; Proposition~\ref{prop:insample} supplies the missing ingredient those
analyses assume, namely that the bootstrap distribution matches the behaviour. The finite
case is standard; the continuous case needs a covering argument over $\Udelta$ and a
Lipschitz condition on $\psi$ in $u$ that has not been stated.

\emph{This is the theorem that formally separates PSMFlows from FB and PSM}: their
operators require action-space concentrability, ours should require only state coverage.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[Compact support and the diffeomorphism tension]
\label{conj:compact}
\NOTPROVED\;
If $\mu(\cdot\mid s)$ is supported on a compact set of Lebesgue measure zero (or on a
lower-dimensional manifold), then no $v_\theta$ satisfying
Assumption~\ref{ass:lipschitz} attains Assumption~\ref{ass:exact}; any sequence
$v_\theta^{(n)}$ with $\muhat^{(n)}\to\mu$ weakly must have
$L_s^{(n)}\to\infty$, equivalently $\inf_u|\det J^{(n)}(s,u)|\to 0$ off the support.

\emph{Why it matters.} This is not a technicality: the regime the paper targets --- narrow,
mode-restricted, near-expert data --- is exactly the regime where $\mu$ concentrates near a
low-dimensional set. The conjecture says Assumption~\ref{ass:exact} is unattainable there
in principle, so every guarantee must be read as approximate, with the approximation
quality worst precisely where the data is thinnest. Quantifying the rate would convert a
caveat into a bound.

\emph{Strategy.} A diffeomorphism preserves Lebesgue-null sets, so $\muhat$ is absolutely
continuous whenever $p_0$ is; the claim is then that convergence to a singular limit forces
Jacobian degeneracy. A Liouville-formula argument on \eqref{eq:liouville} plus a lower
bound on the volume of $\{|\det J|>c\}$ should suffice.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[Coherence of trained flows]
\label{conj:coherence}
\NOTPROVED, \emph{and plausibly not provable.} Whether conditional flow matching produces
$\varepsilon$-coherent decoders (Definition~\ref{def:coherence}) with small $\varepsilon$ is
a property of the trained network and the data, not a theorem. It must be measured
(diagnostic D2). If it fails, Remark~\ref{rem:coherence-role} gives the mitigation.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Open problem: state-distribution shift.}
Nothing in this report addresses it, and the omission should be stated plainly rather than
buried. Corollary~\ref{cor:mass} constrains actions state-by-state; it says nothing about
where the resulting trajectories go. A fixed-index policy $\pi_u$ can chain individually
typical actions into a trajectory that leaves the region $\rho$ covers, and the successor
measure is then queried at states the basis has never seen. This is the state half of
offline distribution shift and it is shared with every offline method; what
Proposition~\ref{prop:insample} removes is the \emph{action} half, which is the half that
temporal-difference backups amplify multiplicatively. Any experimental claim must therefore
report an occupancy-divergence diagnostic (D6) alongside returns, or it will be impossible
to attribute failures correctly.

\paragraph{Open problem: is the successor measure doing work?}
Rung~1 with $K$ samples resembles critic-weighted resampling of behaviour samples (IDQL).
The representation earns its cost only if it beats a per-task critic that ranks the same
$K$ decoded actions. This is an ablation, not a theorem, but it is the one a sceptical
reader will ask for first.
