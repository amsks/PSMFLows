# psmgoal: what works, what breaks

M^{π_z}(s,u,s+) = phi(s,u,s+)ᵀ w(z) + b(s,u,s+). u = latent action, G(s,u) = the action sent to the env.
Cube, mean over the 5 tasks, 500 episodes per task. BC (copy the data) = 0.111.

| hypothesis | test | result |
|---|---|---|
| (phi, b) predicts where the data's own policy goes | Keep phi, b fixed. Fit one w so M describes the data's policy (next latent u' = preimage of the next recorded action). On unseen episodes: does M(s_t, u_t, s+) score the true later state s_{t+k} higher than a random state? Also try b alone (w = 0) and a random w. | Yes, for nearby futures. Fitted w: AUC 1.00 (1 step ahead), 0.985 (10), 0.71 (50). An untuned w(z) does the same (0.99 at 10). b alone: 0.90 at 10. Random w: 0.5. Caveat: the random states are mostly far away, so this is an easy test. It does not show M tells policies apart. |
| (phi, b) can describe policies it never trained on | Make new random policies with a new seed. Keep phi, b fixed and fit a w for each. Is the TD error as low as for the trained policies? | Yes, equal (±0.01). But this is empty: M ignores z (next row), so any policy fits the same. |
| M changes when the policy z changes | Fix the inputs (s, u, s+). Compute M for 256 different z. Does M differ between z, or is it nearly the same for all? Also: how many independent directions do the 256 vectors w(z) use? | No. All w(z) are the same vector (cosine 0.99999999). z explains 1e-13 to 3e-8 of the change in M. The basis encodes one policy. Same with the penalty. |
| M changes when the action u changes | Fix s and s+. Compute M for 64 different u. Does M differ between u? Run on the no-penalty and the penalty checkpoints. | Barely. u explains 0.0001–0.009 of the change in M. With least-squares w: Q changes across u 0.005–0.02× as much as across s (no penalty), 0.5–0.76× (penalty 10, 100). The best u changes with w in 87–100% of states (chance 98%). |
| The diagonal term makes every w(z) the same | On one batch: compare every w(z) with d = mean of phi(s_i, u_i, s'_i) (the row's own next state). Split the gradient on w into the diagonal term and the off-diagonal TD term. | Yes. cos(w(z), d) = 0.99999 for all z. Diagonal gradient points along d (cos 0.9999), TD gradient does not (cos −0.005). M(s_i,u_i,s'_i) = 129 = the max the norms allow (√128·√128 + b). M off the diagonal ≈ 0.005. M has become "is s+ exactly my next state?". Same with the penalty. sd0 only. |
| The same failures appear in zero-shot PSM that works (Walker RND, return 694) | Same measurements on the release's checkpoints: M = psi(s,z,a)ᵀ phi(s+). 3 seeds at 2M, seed 0 at 500k. | Code collapse: yes, also there (cos between codes 0.9999). Action effect: also small (0.4–4% of variance). Differences from psmgoal: diagonal at 0.33–0.38 of its ceiling (psmgoal: 1.0); off-diagonal M ≈ 50 = 1/(1−γ) (psmgoal: ≈ 0); phi uses 84–102 of 128 directions (psmgoal: 1); TD gradient 2.3–3.6× the diagonal one and pulls the diagonal down (psmgoal: smaller and at right angles). |
| psmgoal's loss/code recovers the true M (gridworld, exact M known) | 7×7 grid, real psmgoal networks + loss. One-hot vs continuous (x,y) states, deterministic vs noisy. 2^16 proto codes vs 4 policies. FB-style factorized loss for comparison. 1 seed. | Code correct: with 4 learnable policies, M matches the truth (corr 0.98, slope 0.98). With 2^16 codes, w(z) cannot learn the code→policy hash: one w for all z, phi rank 1, M = the averaged policy's measure (corr 0.98 to that). Same for FB. Spike: only on deterministic continuous states (diag at cap 64/65, off-diag 0.14); there the exact answer is a point mass at s'. Factorized FB form does not spike (off-diag mean ≈ 50). Plain head without cap: diag 277. |
| Fewer policies (4 instead of 2^16) lets w(z) separate and fixes acting | psmgoal_fewpol: max_log_seed = 2, joint form. 3 seeds, 500k. Evals with Lagrangian and least-squares w. | Partial: lstsq w 0.194 at 250k (2 of 3 seeds). 500k and lp pending (training at 280k). |
| Factorizing s+ out (phi = A(s,u)ᵀ f(s+), b = βᵀ f(s+)) removes the spike and fixes acting | psmgoal_factor: 2^16 policies. psmgoal_fewpol_factor: 4 policies. f on the sphere + ortho weight 1. 3 seeds each, 500k. | No. 500k: factor lp 0.012 / lstsq 0.026; fewpol_factor lp 0.012 / lstsq 0.032. Still spikes without a cap: M at own next state ≈ 38, elsewhere ≈ 0.15. |
| M's ranking holds up over the episode | Run real eval episodes (750k, 3 seeds, 5 tasks, least-squares w). At every step t, record the 64 u and their M scores. Does M separate the 64 u as clearly late in the episode as early? Same for successful vs failed episodes. | No drift. In failed episodes the spread of the 64 scores, the near-tie rate (18–22%) and the chosen score are flat from t = 0 to 199. In the first 25 steps, success and failure look the same. The rollouts reproduce the known eval (0.303 vs 0.301). phiᵀw decides the pick on 88% of steps. Whether the pick is right: next row. |
| M's order is right at every step t | Score the same recorded (s_t, 64 u) with the real-reward scalar critic. Does agreement with M drop as t grows? | Reference so far: the psmgoal-reward critic (250k). Rank correlation on rollout states ≈ 0 at every t (−0.09 to 0.10, CIs include 0). Top pick agrees 4–7% (chance 1.6%). Not bad early and worse later: near chance throughout. Real-reward critic pending. |
| M puts the 64 u in the right order | At each state, rank 64 flow-sampled u by M (task w) and by a scalar critic trained on the real reward. Do the two orders agree? Does the top pick agree? All 5 tasks. | No. Dataset states: rank correlation 0.097 ± 0.134, top pick agrees 7% (chance 1.6%). The critic's own two heads agree at 0.82, so the disagreement is M. Reference = psmgoal-reward critic (250k); real-reward critic pending. |
| phi knows where reward is | Average phi(s', u, g) over 32 rewarding goals g. Fit w by least squares so phiᵀw matches the reward r. Does phiᵀw go up and down with r? | Partly. Correlation 0.374. |
| r̂ also ranks non-success states by how close they are to success | For every non-success dataset state: steps until the next success in its episode. Compare r̂ across distances. 5 tasks × 3 seeds. | Only close in. Success vs rest AUC 0.97. Within 10 steps vs over 200 away AUC 0.91. Mean r̂: success −0.83, 1–10 steps −0.89, 11–50 −0.965, 51–200 −0.980, 200+ −0.981, never −0.982. Flat beyond ~50 steps. |
| PSM training is what puts the success signal into f | Same recipe (8 u, 32 anchors, least squares) on the same network with random weights. 3 seeds each, 200k states. | Mostly no. Random weights: corr 0.357, success AUC 0.962, near-vs-far AUC 0.861. Trained: 0.375, 0.972, 0.914. Training adds a little, mostly to near-vs-far. |
| phi uses all D = 128 directions | Compute phi on many (s, u, s+) and count how many independent directions its values spread over. | No. About 1 of 128. |
| The Lagrangian finds a good w* | Same checkpoints. Act with the Lagrangian's w* vs a least-squares w. In both, pick the best of 64 prior u by M. | No. Lagrangian 0.001. Least squares 0.301 (750k), 0.176 (500k). |
| Forcing phi to use all 128 directions helps | Retrain with a penalty that pushes phi to spread over all directions (weights 1, 10, 100). | Spread fixed (127.8 of 128). Scores drop to 0.04–0.07. |
| An actor can follow M | Train an actor u = π(s) to maximize M, with no pull toward the data. | No. 0.001. |
| M can choose among data-like actions | A BC actor proposes 64 u that look like the data. Pick the one with the highest M. | Somewhat. 0.206 (250k). |
| Factored-FB trick works zero-shot in psmgoal | Measure keeps training. Actor(s, w) trained jointly on Q = Σ_j M(s,u,s+_j) · r_w(s+_j) (raw) or with M softmaxed per row (softmax). Tasks: 50% hindsight goals (ridge fit), 50% random w. Test: w by ridge on reward labels, act with the actor. 2 arms × 3 seeds, 500k. | No. raw 0.073 ± 0.036, softmax 0.066 ± 0.049 (500k). Below BC. |
| r̂ = phiᵀw is good enough to learn from | Train a normal scalar critic Q on r̂, and another on the true r. All 5 tasks, 3 seeds, 500k. Compare. | Yes. psmgoal r̂ 0.876 ± 0.005, real r 0.877 ± 0.047 (500k, 500 ep/task, 3 seeds). Same. |
